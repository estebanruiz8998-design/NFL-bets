Every backtestable piece was tested against closing lines at −110 with pre-registered rules, exact binomial statistics, era-drift splits, and independent blind re-implementation of every number. Four manufactured "edges," caught and killed

v1: +4.7% train ROI from a pre-2020 underdog-moneyline regime → −8.6% in validation. v2: +8.2% from corrupted alt-line prices in the dataset → −7.5%. v4: a threshold config that passed consistency gates at +5.4% → −14.6%; a significance gate (t ≥ 2) now blocks the whole class. And the era splits show every Week 1 folk pattern flipping sign between 2002–13 and 2014–25. This is what "profitable backtests" are usually made of.

What survives professionally

1) Bet small or not at all — the market is efficient at the close. 2) If you bet, the discipline layer is the value: price conditions, key numbers, one-position sizing for correlated tickets, fractional-Kelly caps. 3) The futures are researched opinions — you are betting the reasoning, not data. 4) Track every bet against the closing line this season: CLV is the only fast test of whether any live edge exists. Full study: research/strategy-verdict.md in the repo.
